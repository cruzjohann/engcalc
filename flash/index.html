Method, notes and limitations
Same S, compressor / pump (P2 > P1): h2 = h1 + (h2s − h1) / ηs h2s = h(P2, s1) Same S, turbine / expander (P2 < P1): h2 = h1 − ηs · (h1 − h2s) Same H, valve / choke: h2 = h1 (Joule–Thomson) Same P, heater / cooler: outlet from T or Q at P1; q = h2 − h1 Same T: outlet from T1 at the new P Superheat / subcool: ΔTsat = T − Tsat(P)
  • Phase comes from CoolProp's phase result for every flash type. Below the critical pressure the tool also gives Tsat and the superheat (+) or subcooling (−).
  • Cp, Cv, γ, Z, viscosity, conductivity and speed of sound are not defined inside the two-phase region and are shown as “—”. Surface tension is defined only in the two-phase region.
  • Water boils at 99.974 °C at 101.325 kPa on the current temperature scale (ITS-90, IAPWS-95), not exactly 100 °C.
  • Pure and pseudo-pure fluids only. Natural gas is represented by pure methane; real gas compositions shift the phase envelope.
  • Air and refrigerant blends (R404A, R407C, R410A, R507A) are pseudo-pure in CoolProp: one saturation line, no temperature glide.
  • No hydrate, wax or solid checks. Temperatures below the melting line are rejected.
  • Enthalpy and entropy use CoolProp's reference state for each fluid. Compare differences (Δh, Δs) with other software, not absolute values.
  • Machines are adiabatic and modelled by isentropic efficiency only.
